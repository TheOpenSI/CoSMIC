- **Established Processes and Structures**: Providers must have established processes and structures with assigned responsibilities that collectively ensure effective academic governance.

- **Academic Leadership and Expertise**: Academic leadership should be mature, advanced, and relevant to the fields of education being delivered. This includes identifying and implementing good practices in teaching and learning.

- **Scholarship Support**: Sufficient support for scholarship must exist, which informs teaching and contributes to knowledge advancement.

- **Research Capabilities**: Providers are required to undertake research at or above benchmark standards within broad fields of education, leading to new knowledge creation. This applies both generally and specifically to specialized universities.

- **Quality Assurance Mechanisms**: Mature and advanced processes should cover the design, delivery, accreditation, monitoring, institutional quality assurance, review, and improvement of courses of study. Academic integrity must also be maintained.

- **Engagement with Stakeholders**: Providers need to engage with employers, industry, and professions relevant to their offerings.

- **Risk Management**: Corporate governing bodies must manage risks related to the independence and integrity of academic governance structures, avoiding actions that create compliance risks such as major shareholders attending Academic Board meetings.

For detailed confirmation or further guidance on these requirements, you can refer to the TEQSA Act and Higher Education Standards Framework 2021. For more specific advice, contact the University of Canberra's Academic Governance Office or the Research Integrity Unit.
